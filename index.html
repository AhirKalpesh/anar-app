01 Site, Soil & Climate

What an anar orchard needs to establish well and bear reliably.

  • Climate: Semi-arid, with a hot dry summer and a mild winter. Tolerates temperatures from near-freezing to over 45°C, but fruit cracking increases with high humidity near harvest.
  • Soil: Deep, well-drained loam is ideal. Pomegranate tolerates poor and moderately saline/alkaline soils better than most fruit crops, but waterlogging quickly causes root rot — drainage matters more than fertility.
  • Soil pH: 6.5 – 7.5 preferred; can tolerate up to ~8.5 with management.
  • Water: Low overall demand compared to other orchard crops, which is why drip irrigation suits it so well — but it still needs consistent moisture during fruit development to prevent cracking and fruit drop.
Get a soil test before planting if possible — it decides pit preparation, gypsum/lime needs, and your real fertilizer dose far better than any general guide.

02 Planting

  • Spacing: 4.5m × 3m to 5m × 5m depending on variety and mechanization needs. Closer spacing (4m×2m, "high density") is used in some intensive orchards but demands tighter canopy management.
  • Pit size: 60cm × 60cm × 60cm, dug and left open to weather for 2–3 weeks before filling.
  • Pit mix: Top soil + well-rotted farmyard manure (FYM) + a handful of single super phosphate, refilled and watered before transplanting.
  • Best planting window: Onset of monsoon, or spring (Feb–Mar) where irrigation is assured.
  • Common varieties: Bhagwa, Ganesh, Arakta, Mridula, Ruby — Bhagwa dominates commercial export-oriented planting for its deep red rind and arils.

04 Pruning & Training

  • Years 1–2: Train to an open, multi-stem bush of 4–5 well-spaced scaffold branches. Remove all growth below 30–45cm from ground level.
  • Every year after: Remove root suckers and water shoots regularly — pomegranate throws these constantly and they drain vigor from fruiting wood.
  • Post-harvest pruning: Light annual pruning to remove dead, crossing, and diseased wood, and to open the canopy for light and spray penetration.
  • Bahar treatment: Many growers control flowering with a rest-then-irrigate cycle (withholding water, then resuming) to time a specific flowering/harvest window — a technique worth learning from local growers before attempting.

05 Pests & Disease Watch

  • Bacterial blight (Xanthomonas): The single biggest threat to pomegranate — leaf and fruit spotting, cracking. Manage with copper-based sprays, sanitation, and resistant/tolerant planting material; avoid overhead irrigation which spreads it.
  • Fruit borer: Causes internal fruit damage. Use pheromone traps for monitoring, and fruit bagging in high-value orchards.
  • Aphids & thrips: Sap-suckers on new growth and flowers; neem-based sprays for low pressure, need-based insecticide for outbreaks.
  • Anthracnose: Fungal fruit rot, worse in humid/monsoon periods — fungicide spray program during flowering and fruit-set helps.
Always confirm chemical choice, dose and pre-harvest interval with your local agricultural extension office — recommendations vary by region and regulation.

06 Harvest Signs

  • 5–7 months from fruit set to maturity, depending on variety and bahar season.
  • Rind changes to the variety's mature colour (deep red/golden-red for Bhagwa) and turns tight, glossy, and slightly angular in cross-section.
  • A ripe fruit gives a dull, metallic sound when tapped — a hollow-sounding fruit is usually underripe.
  • Harvest by clipping (not pulling) to avoid tearing the crown and stem.

DESIGN Quick System Design Notes

  • Emitters per plant: 2 for young plants, 4–6 online drippers arranged around the drip line once the canopy matures, so the wetted zone tracks the root spread.
  • Lateral spacing: Match to plant row spacing — one lateral per row is standard for single-row planting.
  • Filtration: Always run a screen or disc filter before the laterals — pomegranate drippers clog easily with silt or algae, and blight spreads faster from uneven watering caused by clogged emitters.
  • Flush laterals monthly, and check the tail end of each line for pressure drop.
  • Fertigation: Water-soluble fertilizer can be injected through the same drip system to fold feeding into your irrigation routine — venturi or fertilizer tank injectors are the common low-cost options.
